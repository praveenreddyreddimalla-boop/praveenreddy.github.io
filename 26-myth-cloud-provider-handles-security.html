Myth: "My Cloud Provider Handles All My Security"

Major cloud providers invest enormous resources into securing their physical data centers, hardware, and core infrastructure. It's easy to extend that trust and assume everything running on top of it is automatically secured too — but that's not how the arrangement actually works.

Cloud providers operate on what's widely known as the "shared responsibility model." The provider secures the cloud itself — physical security, the underlying network, the virtualization layer. The customer is responsible for security in the cloud — how you configure access permissions, whether your storage buckets are accidentally left public, whether your applications are patched, and how you manage credentials and encryption.

A large share of publicly reported cloud data exposures haven't been failures of the provider's infrastructure at all — they've been misconfigurations on the customer's side, like a storage bucket set to public access by mistake, or overly broad permissions granted out of convenience.

Key takeaway: moving to the cloud shifts which parts of security you're responsible for; it doesn't remove your responsibility. Knowing exactly where that line sits for your specific provider and service is one of the most important things a team can clarify early.
