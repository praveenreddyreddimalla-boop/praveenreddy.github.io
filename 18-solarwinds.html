SolarWinds: Breaking Down the Supply Chain Attack That Hit Thousands of Orgs

Discovered in December 2020, the SolarWinds attack is one of the most widely studied supply chain compromises on record. Attackers gained access to the build system for SolarWinds' Orion network management software and inserted malicious code directly into a legitimate, digitally signed software update.

That update was then distributed to Orion's customer base — reportedly around 18,000 organizations downloaded the compromised update, including U.S. government agencies and major private companies. Because the malware arrived through an official, signed update from a trusted vendor, it evaded the security tools that would normally have flagged unauthorized software.

Not every organization that downloaded the update was actively exploited further — attackers appear to have selectively chosen higher-value targets from among those with the backdoor installed for deeper follow-on intrusion. But the scale of exposure alone made this one of the most consequential incidents in recent cybersecurity history.

Key takeaway: the attack succeeded specifically by exploiting trust in the software supply chain rather than by breaking through anyone's firewall. It remains a central case study for why "verify, don't just trust" now extends to your vendors' own build pipelines.
