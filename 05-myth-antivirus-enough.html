Myth: "I Have Antivirus, So I'm Safe"

Antivirus software is genuinely useful — it's very good at catching known malware signatures and increasingly good at flagging suspicious behavior. But treating it as a complete safety net is where the myth breaks down.

Antivirus generally can't stop you from voluntarily typing your password into a convincing fake login page. It can't stop a scammer from talking you into a wire transfer over the phone. And it struggles against brand-new malware variants that haven't been seen and catalogued yet, which is exactly what attackers are constantly producing.

A more realistic mental model: antivirus is one layer in a stack that also needs strong unique passwords, multi-factor authentication, regular software updates, and — most importantly — a healthy skepticism toward unexpected messages, links, and requests for money or credentials.

Key takeaway: keep your antivirus updated and running, but don't let it lower your guard. The majority of successful breaches involve tricking a person, not defeating a piece of software.
