5 Red Flags That Give Away a Phishing Email Every Time

Even as phishing gets more sophisticated, a handful of patterns still show up consistently and are worth training yourself to notice.

Urgency or fear: messages pushing you to act immediately — "your account will be suspended," "unusual sign-in detected" — are designed to short-circuit careful thinking. Mismatched sender details: the display name might say your bank, but the actual email address behind it often doesn't match the real domain — always check the full address, not just the name shown.

Requests that bypass normal process: a request to change payment details, approve an unusual invoice, or buy gift cards, especially if it claims to come from an executive, is a classic pattern regardless of how well-written the email is. Links that don't match their destination: hovering over a link (without clicking) shows the real URL — if it doesn't match the text or the company it claims to be from, that's a strong signal. Unexpected attachments: especially ones you weren't expecting from someone you do know, since compromised accounts are often used to spread malware to their own contacts.

Key takeaway: no single red flag is proof on its own, but two or three together are a strong signal to stop and verify through a separate channel before acting.
