The AWS us-east-1 Outage: A Lesson in Single Points of Failure

Amazon Web Services' us-east-1 region, based in Northern Virginia, is one of the oldest and most heavily used regions in AWS's cloud. Because of that, it has also become something of an unofficial default for many companies — including some of AWS's own internal services — which means outages there have historically had an outsized ripple effect across the internet.

When incidents have hit that region, they've knocked out or degraded services for a wide range of well-known apps and websites simultaneously, even ones that had no obvious business relationship to each other — the common thread was simply that they all depended on the same underlying region.

The broader lesson for engineering teams is about single points of failure: relying on one region, one provider, or one dependency — no matter how reliable it usually is — creates a ceiling on how available your own service can ever be. True resilience typically requires multi-region or multi-provider redundancy, with a real, tested failover path rather than just a plan on paper.

Key takeaway: "but it's a major cloud provider, it won't go down" is not a resilience strategy. Ask what happens to your service specifically when your primary region does go down — because eventually, it will.
