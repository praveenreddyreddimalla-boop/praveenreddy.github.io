Myth: "Small Businesses Aren't Targets"

It's a common assumption that cybercriminals only go after large corporations with deep pockets. In reality, small and mid-sized businesses are frequently targeted, and multiple industry reports over the years have found they make up a substantial share of overall attack targets.

The logic from an attacker's perspective is simple: small businesses often have valuable data — customer records, payment information, intellectual property — but typically invest far less in security staff and tooling than large enterprises. That combination of "has something worth stealing" and "is easier to breach" makes them an efficient target, not an unlikely one.

Many attacks against small businesses are also automated and opportunistic rather than personally targeted — scanning tools look for any exposed, unpatched system on the internet, with no regard for the size of the company behind it. Being "too small to notice" doesn't apply to a script that's scanning millions of IP addresses.

Key takeaway: attackers don't need you to be famous, only reachable and unprotected. Basic defenses — MFA, patching, backups, and staff awareness training — go a long way precisely because so many small businesses skip them.
