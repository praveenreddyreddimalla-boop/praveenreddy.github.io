Turn On MFA Everywhere: A 60-Second How-To

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) adds a second proof of identity beyond your password — a code from an app, a tap on your phone, or a hardware key. Industry research from major identity providers has repeatedly found that MFA blocks the vast majority of automated account-takeover attempts, because a stolen password alone stops being enough to get in.

To turn it on: open the security or login settings of an account, look for "Two-factor authentication" or "Multi-factor authentication," and choose an authenticator app (like Google Authenticator or Authy) over SMS text codes where possible — SMS can be intercepted through SIM-swap attacks, while an authenticator app cannot.

Start with the accounts that would hurt the most if compromised: your primary email (since it can reset almost everything else), your bank, and any work accounts with access to sensitive systems. From there, work outward to social media and shopping accounts.

Key takeaway: MFA takes about a minute to set up per account and remains one of the single highest-impact security habits available to anyone, regardless of technical background.
