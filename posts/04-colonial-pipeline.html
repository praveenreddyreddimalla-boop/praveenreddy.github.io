Colonial Pipeline: When a Cyberattack Becomes an Availability Crisis

In May 2021, Colonial Pipeline — which carries a large share of the fuel supply for the U.S. East Coast — was hit by ransomware that encrypted its billing systems. Even though the industrial control systems that actually pump fuel weren't directly compromised, the company proactively shut down pipeline operations because it couldn't confirm how far the intrusion had spread and couldn't reliably bill customers for fuel delivered.

The result was days of fuel shortages, panic buying, and price spikes across multiple states — all triggered by an attack that hit the business side of the network, an entry point widely reported to trace back to a single compromised password on a VPN account without multi-factor authentication.

This is the clearest possible illustration of why availability belongs in the same conversation as security. The attackers didn't need to touch operational technology to cause a national fuel disruption; they only needed to make the company doubt the integrity of its own systems. Uncertainty was enough to force a shutdown.

Key takeaway: your "non-critical" systems are only non-critical until an incident forces you to distrust everything connected to them. Segmenting networks and maintaining a tested incident response plan can mean the difference between an IT problem and a regional crisis.
