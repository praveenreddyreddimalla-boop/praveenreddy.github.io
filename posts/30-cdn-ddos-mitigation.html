CDNs & DDoS Mitigation: How Sites Stay Up Under Attack

A Content Delivery Network (CDN) is a distributed network of servers spread across many geographic locations that cache and serve a website's content from whichever location is closest to each visitor. The original design goal was speed, but CDNs have become one of the most important tools for availability and DDoS resilience too.

Because a CDN absorbs traffic across many distributed points of presence rather than one origin server, a flood of malicious traffic gets spread thin across that whole network instead of overwhelming a single server directly. Many CDN providers also layer in dedicated DDoS mitigation — automatically detecting abnormal traffic patterns and filtering or rate-limiting them before they ever reach your actual infrastructure.

This is why, closing out the month where we started with the Colonial Pipeline and Mirai incidents, so much of modern availability engineering isn't about making your own servers unbreakable — it's about not exposing them directly to the raw internet in the first place, and instead putting resilient, purpose-built infrastructure in front of them.

Key takeaway: a CDN is often one of the highest-leverage, lowest-effort additions to an availability strategy — improving speed for legitimate users while absorbing a meaningful share of malicious traffic before it ever becomes your problem.
