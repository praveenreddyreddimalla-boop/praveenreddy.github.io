Disaster Recovery vs Business Continuity: What's Actually the Difference?

Disaster Recovery (DR) and Business Continuity (BC) are closely related and often used interchangeably, but they answer different questions, and mature organizations plan for both separately.

Disaster Recovery is specifically about restoring IT systems and data after a disruption — getting servers, applications, and databases back online, ideally within a defined Recovery Time Objective (how long you can be down) and Recovery Point Objective (how much data you can afford to lose). It's fundamentally a technical, systems-focused discipline.

Business Continuity is broader: it's about keeping the overall business functioning during a disruption, which includes IT recovery but also covers things like where staff work from if a building is inaccessible, how customers get served if the primary system is down, and which processes have manual fallback procedures.

Key takeaway: DR is a subset of BC. You can have a perfect technical recovery plan and still fail as a business if no one has thought through how staff, customers, and processes keep functioning while that recovery is happening.
