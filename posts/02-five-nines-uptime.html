What Does '99.999% Uptime' Actually Mean in Minutes of Downtime?

"Five nines" gets thrown around as the gold standard of reliability, but the percentage alone doesn't mean much until you translate it into time. That's the number that actually matters to a business: how many minutes can go wrong before customers notice.

Here's the breakdown, from loosest to strictest: 99% uptime allows about 3.65 days of downtime a year. 99.9% ("three nines") allows roughly 8.76 hours. 99.99% ("four nines") allows about 52.6 minutes. And 99.999% ("five nines") allows only about 5.26 minutes a year — less time than a single coffee break.

Chasing five nines isn't free. Every additional nine typically means more redundant infrastructure, more automated failover, more monitoring, and more operational discipline — and the cost tends to rise faster than the improvement in the percentage. Most organizations pick a target based on what an outage actually costs them, not on what sounds impressive in a pitch deck.

Key takeaway: before promising an uptime number, translate it into minutes per year and ask honestly whether your architecture and budget can actually support it.
